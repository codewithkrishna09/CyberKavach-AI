The Screen-Share Epidemic: Weaponizing AnyDesk

How fraudsters bypass the world's best Antivirus software by simply asking you to let them in. The psychology and mechanics of remote-desktop banking fraud.

CyberKavach Threat Labs
Behavioral Analysis Division

1. Why Antivirus Fails Here

When we talk about malware, we usually imagine a hidden virus sneaking into a phone and breaking its code. But what happens when the software stealing your money is 100% legal, digitally signed, and available on the Google Play Store?

Apps like AnyDesk, TeamViewer, and RustDesk are legitimate tools built for IT support. Because they are legal, no traditional Antivirus will flag them as malicious. Cybercriminals know this. Instead of hacking your device, they hack your psychology.

2. The "KYC Update" Lure

The scam almost always begins with a manufactured crisis. A phone call from a supposed "Bank Executive," "BSNL Electricity Board," or "Jio Customer Support."

"Sir, your electricity connection will be cut at 9:00 PM tonight because your last bill update failed. Please install the 'Quick Support' app from the Play Store so I can guide you to fix it."

3. Handing Over the Keys (The 9-Digit Code)

Once the victim installs the app (AnyDesk), the application generates a unique 9-digit address. The scammer patiently asks the user to read this number aloud.

The moment the scammer enters this number on their own computer, a popup appears on the victim's phone asking for permission to share the screen. Scammers use highly persuasive language like "Sir, just click 'Accept' so the KYC server can sync your details."

4. The "Black Screen" and OTP Interception

Now the scammer can see everything the victim sees. The scammer asks the victim to make a "test transaction of ₹1" to verify the KYC. While the victim enters their UPI PIN, the scammer is silently watching.

Some advanced remote desktop tools even have a "Privacy Mode" or "Black Screen" feature. Once connected, the scammer turns the victim's screen completely black. The victim thinks their phone has hung or rebooted, but in reality, the scammer is navigating through the phone, opening the banking app, and reading the incoming OTPs directly from the screen.
